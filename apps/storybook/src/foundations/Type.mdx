# Type

A warm, slightly eccentric serif over a neutral grotesque. Piazzolla carries an
optical size axis from 8 to 30 and it is left to run automatically, so the face
redraws itself between a card title and a page heading rather than being scaled.
Sofia Sans is the quiet half by design — it carries every label, value, button
and error, and its job is to recede so the serif is the voice.

The two were chosen for each other. Measured at a common size their x-heights sit
within 1.2 percent — 0.482em against 0.488em — which is why they hold together
where they meet, in a card title above its description.

All three faces are continuously variable — Piazzolla 100–900, Sofia Sans 1–1000,
Lilex 100–900 — so
every role carries the weight it needs rather than the nearest hundred. Both are
local WOFF2 shipped inside `@jflamb/keycaps-tokens`; nothing is fetched at
runtime, including on this page.

## The five roles

Each sample below is rendered in the face and settings it actually ships with,
and the specification underneath is read back off the rendered element.

<TypeRoles />

<Rule name="The Two Voice Rule.">
  Piazzolla speaks in page headings and card titles. Sofia Sans does everything
  else — every label, every value, every button, every error. Lilex speaks only
  in code. Piazzolla creeping into body copy breaks the system.
</Rule>

<Rule name="The Optical Weight Rule.">
  Roles carry the weight they need, not the nearest hundred. Apparent stroke
  weight falls as size falls, so Display sits at 580 and Title at 640 — the same
  intent at twice the size apart, bracketing 600 rather than sharing it. Label
  is 580, Micro 760, Body an untuned 400. Dark mode subtracts 20 from every one
  of them, because light glyphs on a dark ground irradiate and read heavier.
</Rule>

<Rule name="The Optical Sizing Rule.">
  Piazzolla's <code>opsz</code> axis is never pinned. It runs on
  <code>font-optical-sizing: auto</code>, so the face draws itself for the size
  it is set at — heavier detail and looser fit at a card title, finer at a page
  heading. Pinning it to one value would trade away the small-size drawing that
  makes the Title role work, which is the reason this face was chosen.
</Rule>

## Type tokens

Sizes, weights, and leading are tokenized. Display sizes are not: the surface
decides how large a heading is, the face does not.

## Headings and the consuming app

`@jflamb/keycaps-tokens/base.css` sets the display face on `h1` and `h2` through
`:where(h1, h2)` — deliberately zero specificity, so a consuming app's own
heading styles win without needing `!important`. That is the contract, and it is
why this documentation site restates the display face in its own stylesheet
rather than raising the specificity of the token layer.

If your app wants Piazzolla on `h3` too, set it yourself; Keycaps will not fight
you for it.

<Rule name="Don't introduce a third typeface, or let Piazzolla into body copy." tone="dont">
  And don't pin the optical size axis per surface. If a surface needs a
  different voice, the surface is wrong, not the axis.
</Rule>
