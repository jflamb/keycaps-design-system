# Type

A softened transitional serif over a humanist sans. Fraunces ships at `opsz 11`
and `SOFT 80` — a low optical size with softened terminals, which keeps it warm
and slightly bookish rather than sharp and editorial. Nunito Sans has rounded
terminals and open apertures, so the pairing reads friendly at small sizes
without becoming informal.

Both faces are local WOFF2 shipped inside `@jflamb/keycaps-tokens`. Nothing is
fetched at runtime, including on this page.

## The five roles

Each sample below is rendered in the face and settings it actually ships with,
and the specification underneath is read back off the rendered element.

<Rule name="The Two Voice Rule.">
  Fraunces speaks in page headings and card titles. Nunito Sans does everything
  else — every label, every value, every button, every error. A third face, or
  Fraunces creeping into body copy, breaks the system.
</Rule>

<Rule name="The Optical Constant Rule.">
  Fraunces is always set at <code>opsz 11, SOFT 80</code>. The variable axes are a
  brand constant, not a per-surface control — they live in a single token,
  <code>--kc-font-variation-display</code>, so there is one place to read them and
  one place they could ever change. Changing them changes the voice.
</Rule>

## Type tokens

Sizes, weights, and leading are tokenized. Display sizes are not: the surface
decides how large a heading is, the face does not.

## Headings and the consuming app

`@jflamb/keycaps-tokens/base.css` sets the display face on `h1` and `h2` through
`:where(h1, h2)` — deliberately zero specificity, so a consuming app's own
heading styles win without needing `!important`. That is the contract, and it is
why this documentation site restates the display face in its own stylesheet
rather than raising the specificity of the token layer.

If your app wants Fraunces on `h3` too, set it yourself; Keycaps will not fight
you for it.

<Rule name="Don't introduce a third typeface, or let Fraunces into body copy." tone="dont">
  And don't override the variable axes per surface. If a surface needs a
  different voice, the surface is wrong, not the axes.
</Rule>
