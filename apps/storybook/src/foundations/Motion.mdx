# Motion

The system contains real physical feedback and it spends it almost nowhere. One
gesture moves; everything else holds still. If everything moved, the press would
mean nothing.

## Hold a key

The left pane is the system's motion. The right pane is the same two keys with
the reduced-motion substitution applied. Press and hold either.

<PressLab />

A primary key travels `3px` on press while its `4px` bottom edge compresses to
`1px`, over `120ms`. **The two values are coupled**: the cap descends exactly as
far as its wall shrinks, so the wall reads as absorbing the travel rather than the
whole object sliding. That is the illusion — a cap hitting the plate beneath it.
Change one without the other and the object stops being solid.

**The press is transform-only, and the constraint is the interesting part.**
`min-block-size` pins the border box, so the wall compresses inside a box that
never resizes. Animating the height instead would hold the outer edge still on
paper; in practice it relayouts every frame and re-centers the key inside any
container that centers its items — measured as a 1.1px upward flick followed by a
slide landing 1.5px low. A wobble, not a press. Motion that has to move a layout
property is usually the wrong motion.

When motion is suppressed the travel is removed and the wall stays at its full
`4px`. A primary key instead fills with `--kc-color-key-edge`. The press is still
legible; it is expressed in material instead of movement.

<Rule name="The Pressable Edge Rule.">
  A bottom edge promises travel. Anything wearing a <code>border-block-end</code>
  heavier than its other borders must depress when activated. A static element
  with a weighted bottom edge is a false affordance.
</Rule>

<Rule name="Don't change one press value without the other." tone="dont">
  <code>--kc-press-travel</code> and <code>--kc-press-edge-width</code> are one
  gesture. Adjusting either alone breaks the key's physics.
</Rule>

## Every value, and what it becomes

<MotionValues />

Reduced motion is not handled in component CSS. Travel, edge compression, chevron
turn, durations, and the pressed key face are all tokens, so
`@jflamb/keycaps-tokens` owns the entire substitution and no component can opt out
of it or forget to implement it.

## The motion contract

Keycaps follows the operating system's `prefers-reduced-motion` setting by
default. An app that offers its own in-product motion preference sets it
explicitly on the root element, mirroring the theme contract:

```html
<html data-kc-motion="reduce">
<html data-kc-motion="full">
```

`reduce` forces the substitution on. `full` opts a user back into motion even
when the system asks to reduce it — offer that only behind a deliberate control,
never as a default. With no attribute, the system preference wins. Keycaps writes
no cookies or browser storage for this, exactly as with
<DocLink to="foundations-consuming-keycaps--guidance">`data-theme`</DocLink>.

The **Motion** control in this site's toolbar sets that same attribute on this
document — it is the shipped contract, not a documentation-only simulation.
