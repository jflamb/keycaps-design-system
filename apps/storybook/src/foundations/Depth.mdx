# Depth & elevation

Depth is physical in light and tonal in dark, and this asymmetry is deliberate.
A shadow on a dark ground reads as grime; a lighter surface reads as closer.

## The same two objects, both physics

The panes below are the real components. Keycaps' theme contract is root-scoped —
`data-theme` belongs on the document element and nowhere else — so this page
reads the dark theme's own declarations and applies them to one subtree rather
than pretending the contract is something it is not.

<Rule name="The Light Lifts, Dark Layers Rule.">
  Cast shadow belongs to light mode. In dark mode, depth comes from surface tone
  alone. Adding a plate shadow to a dark surface is a defect, not an enhancement.
</Rule>

<Rule name="The Overlay Exception Rule.">
  Only genuinely detached surfaces — popovers, select menus, dialogs — cast in
  both themes. If it rests on the plate, it does not cast in dark.
</Rule>

## The two shadows

<ValueTable caption="Shadow tokens, resolved in both themes" prefix="--kc-shadow-" />

`--kc-shadow-plate` is a two-layer shadow: a 1px contact shadow at 6% and a 28px
ambient at 10%, both tinted with the graphite ink rather than black — which is
what keeps a raised card warm instead of gray. In dark it resolves to `none`.

`--kc-shadow-overlay` survives in both themes and deepens in dark, because a
popover is genuinely detached from the plate rather than resting on it, and
detachment has to be legible under both physics.

Under `forced-colors: active` both resolve to `none` and depth is carried
entirely by system border colors. Switch the **Forced colors** control in the
toolbar to see it.

<Rule name="Don't drift toward borderless flat minimalism." tone="dont">
  Shadowless cards, seamless surfaces, objects that dissolve into the page.
  Keycaps is built on things being seated and visibly distinct from their ground.
  This is the system's confirmed anti-reference.
</Rule>
